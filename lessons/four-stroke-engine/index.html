    How a Four-Stroke Engine Works

    This manual explains how a four-stroke engine changes the energy in fuel into rotation. It shows the parts, the four strokes, and the ideal cycle. You can move a model of the engine and change its compression ratio.

    • Level: Intro
    • Time: 20 min
    • Goal: Explain one cycle
    The one idea

    The engine does four strokes in two turns of the crankshaft, but only one stroke, the power stroke, gives work to the crankshaft.

    Summary

    A four-stroke engine burns a mixture of fuel and air in a closed cylinder. The hot gas pushes a piston. The piston turns a crankshaft. The engine does this in a cycle of four strokes.

    • A stroke is one movement of the piston, up or down.
    • Two valves control the flow of gas into and out of the cylinder.
    • The ratio of the largest volume to the smallest volume controls the ideal efficiency.

    The parts

    Learn the names of these parts first. This manual uses the same name for each part in all chapters.

    PartFunction
    PistonMoves up and down in the cylinder.
    Connecting rodTransmits the force from the piston to the crankshaft.
    CrankshaftChanges the linear movement of the piston into rotation.
    Intake valveLets the fuel-air mixture into the cylinder.
    Exhaust valveLets the burned gas out of the cylinder.
    Spark plugMakes a spark that starts the combustion.
    Check-in · The parts

    Which part changes the up-and-down movement of the piston into rotation?

    The connecting rod pushes on an offset pin of the crankshaft. Thus, the crankshaft turns.

    Each stroke has one function. The valves and the spark plug operate at a different time in each stroke. Figure 3 shows the four strokes in their sequence.

    Note

    The highest position of the piston is top dead center (TDC). The lowest position is bottom dead center (BDC). Each stroke goes from one of these positions to the other.

    Check-in · The four strokes

    In which stroke does the hot gas push the piston down?

    In the intake stroke, the piston also moves down, but the crankshaft pulls it. Only in the power stroke does the gas push the piston.

    Check-in · Turn the crankshaft

    How many turns of the crankshaft make one full cycle?

    Each stroke is a half turn of the crankshaft. Four half turns are equal to two turns.

    The ideal cycle

    Engineers show the cycle on a graph of pressure against volume. This graph shows the ideal Otto cycle. The area inside the loop is equal to the work from one cycle.

    The compression ratio r is the largest volume divided by the smallest volume. The ideal efficiency is η = 1 − 1/rγ−1. A real engine has a lower efficiency because of heat loss and friction.

    Caution

    Do not increase the compression ratio of a real petrol engine without data. A high compression ratio can cause knock, which can damage the piston.

    Calculate the ideal efficiency

    1. Find the compression ratio r of the engine.
    2. Calculate r to the power of 0.4.
    3. Divide 1 by the result of step 2.
    4. Subtract the result of step 3 from 1.
    5. Multiply the result by 100 to get a percentage.
    Check-in · The ideal cycle

    Use the steps above. What is the ideal efficiency for a compression ratio of 8?

    8 to the power of 0.4 is 2.297. 1 divided by 2.297 is 0.435. 1 − 0.435 is 0.565, thus 56.5%. Answer C is 1 − 1/8, which does not use the power.

    Glossary

    This page uses these technical nouns. Each name has one meaning on this page.

    bottom dead center
    The lowest position of the piston in the cylinder.
    combustion
    The fast burn of the fuel-air mixture that makes hot gas.
    compress
    To decrease the volume of a gas, which increases its pressure.
    compression ratio
    The largest volume of the cylinder divided by the smallest volume.
    connecting rod
    The rod that attaches the piston to the crankshaft.
    crankshaft
    The shaft that changes the linear movement of the piston into rotation.
    four-stroke engine
    An engine that does one cycle in four strokes of the piston.
    knock
    Combustion that starts in the wrong place or at the wrong time and makes a sharp sound.
    Otto cycle
    The ideal cycle of a spark ignition engine, with heat addition at constant volume.
    stroke
    One movement of the piston from one dead center to the other.
    top dead center
    The highest position of the piston in the cylinder.
    valve timing
    The crank angles at which each valve opens and closes.
